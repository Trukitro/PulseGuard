Help

What PulseGuard does

PulseGuard watches your RAM, CPU, and GPU usage in the background and flags sudden spikes -- not just high totals -- telling you which process caused one when it happens. Disk and network activity are also shown for reference (no spike detection on those yet).

The RAM / CPU / GPU rings

  • The big number is the current percentage; the smaller number under it is the absolute amount (GB for RAM, VRAM GB for GPU).
  • Green: normal, well below your ceiling.
  • Amber: past the "Warning" threshold (a % of your ceiling) -- worth keeping an eye on.
  • Red: within the "danger margin" of your ceiling, or an actual spike is currently active.
  • Click a ring to focus the Timeline chart on that resource, with accurate spike markers for it.

Timeline chart

Shows the selected resource's history with red dots marking detected spikes. Toggle "Show all" to overlay RAM/CPU/GPU together as percentages on one axis (click any legend entry to hide/show that line) -- spike markers aren't shown in this combined view since RAM's spikes are recorded in GB, not %, and plotting them here would misrepresent the scale.

Disk & Network cards

Live read/write and download/upload rates, auto-scaled from B/s up to GB/s.

Top processes panel

By default shows the processes responsible for the last detected spike. Toggle "Live view" to instead continuously show the current top processes for whichever resource is selected, regardless of spikes.

Live indicator

The dot next to the logo pulses on every real update and reads Live, Stale (data hasn't refreshed as recently as expected), or Disconnected. A "Reconnect" link appears when it isn't Live, to force a fresh connection instead of waiting. Hover it to see the current refresh interval.

Mini mode

Collapses the app to a small always-on-top widget showing just the three rings, for passive monitoring while you work. Click the corner icon to restore the full window.

History

Browse what happened over the last 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days -- a combined chart plus a list of every spike in that range.

System tray

Closing the window minimizes PulseGuard to the tray rather than quitting -- it keeps monitoring and notifying in the background. Right-click the tray icon to reopen the window, pause notifications, or exit for real.

Settings

  • Ceiling: the usage level (%) that counts as "full" for that resource.
  • Delta threshold: how much a resource has to jump within the detection window to count as a spike, even if still under ceiling.
  • Ring colors: "Warning past" is a % of ceiling; "Red within" is how many percentage points before ceiling counts as danger.
  • Notifications / Start with Windows: self-explanatory toggles. Notifications also auto-pause automatically while a fullscreen app (like a game) is in front.
  • Chart history: how much data the Timeline keeps in memory.
  • Poll interval: how often PulseGuard samples your system, in seconds.
